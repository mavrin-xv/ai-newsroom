# The 4 changes that took 14 firms from a 9-day month-end close to 5 (with the checklist)

Nine working days. That was one pilot firm's month-end close, and the owner saw the month's numbers on calendar day 10.

Across the 14 firms in our [pilot](https://example.org/pilot-report), the median close went from 9 working days to 5. None of the four changes below needs new software.

## Why the close drags

The close is slow because work piles up at the end. Receipts arrive late, the bank match covers four weeks at once, and the accountant gets the questions last.

In the pilot, 31% of expense lines had no receipt on day 1 of close. Every one of those is a message to a colleague, a wait and a reminder, all in the same week.

Each change below moves one piece of that pile earlier in the month.

## 1. Receipts on the day

The rule: a receipt is photographed the day the money is spent, or the card is paused.

It sounds harsh. The reasoning is that the cost lands on the person who can fix it.

Check the count every Tuesday, in the same slot as the bank match.

## 2. The bank match, weekly

Pick one slot, Tuesday morning for example. Match everything that cleared since the last slot.

Anything you cannot match goes on a short list with a name next to it. The person named answers before the next slot.

We did not time the weekly slot in the pilot. Time yours for the first month and keep the number.

## 3. One checklist, same order

Copy this:

```text
[ ] all receipts in
[ ] bank matched to the last day of the month
[ ] payroll journal posted
[ ] accruals for unbilled work
[ ] accountant's list sent (it goes out on day 2, before the lines above are done)
```

Payroll and accruals depend on a clean bank match. Do them first and you do them twice.

Write the day each line was done. After two months, fix the step that took longest.

## 4. The accountant's list on day 2

Your accountant cannot start until they have your questions. Send what you know and what you do not on day 2, not everything on day 6.

Three parts, one page: lines you could not match, entries you are unsure how to book, anything unusual this month.

## What gets better

The answers from earlier months become rules your team follows. The list shortens. Refunds and transfers between your own accounts each get a fixed rule the first time you see them.

## Where this does not fit

Under a few dozen bank lines a month, in our judgement, a weekly match is overhead. Match monthly and keep the checklist.

If your accountant closes the books for you, changes 1 and 4 still help.
